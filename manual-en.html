User manualSolidMaker · Beta

Welcome to SolidMaker.

Thank you for choosing SolidMaker. This manual goes with you from the first time you open the program to your first manufactured part.

Every screen, menu and tool appears here with a real screenshot of the program and an explanation of its fields: what each one means, which unit it takes and its default value. If at any point you are not sure what a button does, look it up in the tool index: every tool has its own link. The screenshots show the Spanish interface; SolidMaker can switch its language in Settings.

02 / PolarCADGet to know your screen

The PolarCAD screen.

When you open PolarCAD you will see this screen. It has five areas, and it is worth knowing them before you draw the first line.

Area 1

Tool ribbon

At the top. Four tabs hold every drawing, modelling, assembly and inspection tool.

 Area 2

Module column and console

On the left. It switches modules and opens the settings of each tool and of the object.

 Area 3

3D viewer

The centre. Views, orientation cube and the working grid.

 Area 4

Project manager

On the right. Your projects, files and the parts library.

 Area 5

Status bar

At the bottom. Active plane, cursor position, selection and open file.
Area 1

The tool ribbon

The ribbon groups the tools into four tabs, in the order you normally work. When you press a tab, the row below changes its tools.

Sketch: drawing, curves, dimensions and relations, and stroke editing. Model: features, cut, finish, transformations, patterns, reference, sheet metal and machining.

Assemble: joints and actuators. Inspect: measure and material. Document, Undo and Redo always stay on the left; Settings, Grid settings and Help on the right.

Buttons with a ▾ arrow open a menu. Below you will find the full index: each name takes you to the explanation of that tool.

Area 2

Module column and tool console

The vertical column on the left edge switches modules: CAD, Laboratory, Machining, Systems, Polar slicer and Analysis.

Next to it the Tool console opens. There you will find the settings of each tool and of the selected object. Its sections are: Properties, Joints, Interferences, Relations, Material, Variables, References, Measurement and 2D drawing.

When you choose a feature from the ribbon, its fields appear here with the Apply and Cancel buttons.

Area 3

The 3D viewer

At the top left are the Iso 3D, Top, Side and Front views; the eye shows or hides elements and P switches the projection. Below, three buttons: select, lock and layers.

The cube in the top right corner shows the orientation and lets you turn the view by clicking its faces. The background grid is 5 mm per square (change it in Grid settings).

Area 4

The Manager and the Project tab

The right panel opens with the vertical Project tab. It has three tabs: Files, Project and Library.

The Project tab shows all your projects as a tree, like Windows Explorer: each folder opens with [+] and holds its parts, assemblies, exports and versions. + import brings in files from your computer and + project creates a new one. Hovering over a project shows the buttons to create a folder, rename and delete.

The Library tab holds 1,535 standard parts in 81 families (screws, nuts, bearings, extrusions, motors) ready to insert.

Area 5

The status bar

The bottom line of the viewer tells you where you are at all times: active Plane, Pos (X, Y, Z cursor coordinates in mm), Sel (what is selected), open File, status of the exact-geometry Kernel and the Grid size. Below, the work mode (Design) and the current section.

Module 02 / MachinBack to modules ↑

Machin.

Machine simulator and control: it loads an S-code (.scode) from the polar slicer, plays it back in 3D on the active machine and sends it to the real machine over Bluetooth, WiFi or USB.

Areas: top bar (Help, Machine), left panel (coordinates, jog, hardware limits, ESP32, communication, θ disc), 3D viewer with the timeline below, right panel (Decoder, instructions, single-layer demo, speed, run and stops).

Full screen, just openedHelp menu (keyboard shortcuts)Machine menuLeft panel scrolled: ESP32, Communication, θ disc and real machine settingsRight panel: Decoder in manual mode (paste BP/FD data)
First steps
  1. In the side menu go to Machining: Machin opens with the default machine, PolarONE.
  2. In the Machine menu choose another Stonward machine, or use «Acquire new machine» to install a .zip package.
  3. In PolarSlicer press «Send to Machin», or open a G-code or S-code file.
  4. Press play to simulate the path with the real kinematics. The traffic light warns you if any point is out of reach.
  5. To move the real machine, connect the controller over USB and open communication in the left panel.

PolarSlicer.

Prepares an STL part for printing on the polar machine: it places it on the disc, slices it into layers and generates the S-code (polar kinematics θ, r, z), which you download or send to Machin.

The left panel is an accordion: each screenshot shows one section open with its subsections. The final «Slice and generate S-code» button becomes active once a part is loaded.

Full screen, just openedWork volumeParts on the discNozzleExtruderHow to printSlicingInfillSupportsResultLog
First steps
  1. In the side menu go to Slicer: PolarSlicer opens.
  2. Choose the machine in the selector next to «Send to Machin». The output switches between G-code and S-code by itself, according to its kinematics.
  3. Import the part as STL and place it on the disc or the bed.
  4. Set the nozzle, extruder, infill, supports and slicing mode (planar, diagonal or elliptic).
  5. Slice, check layer by layer with the slider and send the result to Machin.
